3.1.3 Critique

The speaker, Alex Gurovich, offers a sophisticated and self-aware approach to macro trading. His logic is generally sound, rooted in strategic thinking developed over decades.

Strengths: * Self-Correction and Learning from Mistakes: Gurovich openly acknowledges his “big mistake” in not fully internalizing the breakdown of the secular bond bull market, demonstrating intellectual honesty and adaptability, which is crucial for long-term success. * Contrarian Thinking: His high-conviction view that US rates will fall to zero, despite current inflation fears, is a well-articulated contrarian thesis backed by a concurrent necessity framework (deteriorating job market + high real rates = disinflation). * Focus on Strategic Principles: He rightly emphasizes what makes a good trade (ex-ante characteristics) rather than just predicting market direction, which aligns with robust portfolio management. * Realistic View on AI: His long-term perspective on AI’s disruptive potential, including its impact on labor and discretionary trading, provides a valuable and sobering outlook. He is also pragmatic about how to leverage it or align with its potential influence on markets. * Nuanced Risk Management: His opposition to blanket mechanical stop-losses and preference for options as portfolio risk management tools (especially when views are highly confident but could be catastrophically wrong) highlights a deep understanding of risk beyond simple thresholds.

Areas for Further Consideration/Critique: * Subjectivity of “Giving Up”: While understandable that traders sometimes “just run with the market” or “give up on a trade,” the decision to exit a position “because it didn’t feel really good anymore to me” can be subjective and prone to emotional biases, even for experienced traders. He does mention “other reasons” to get out, but these are not explicitly detailed. * Lack of Fundamental Valuation for Precious Metals: He candidly admits, “I really don’t know what drives silver to $115 an ounce or whatever it is.” While his strategy to “run with the market” during momentum phases is valid, it highlights a reliance on technicals and market psychology without underlying fundamental support, which can be risky for longer-term positions if momentum shifts abruptly. * General Lack of Specific Price Targets/Key Levels: Apart from the 4% yield for JGBs and a speculative $10,000 for gold (AI-driven), specific entry, stop-loss, or take-profit levels are largely absent. This aligns with his preference for strategic, longer-term views and less mechanical trading but makes direct “actionable intelligence” in terms of entry/exit points less concrete for immediate application.

Critique Mentioned by the Speaker: * His own mistake: Not fully internalizing the breakdown of the secular bull bond market after it had a parabolic rally in 2020. * Skepticism about AI: He views skepticism about AI’s power as “laughable,” having foreseen its transformative impact since 2016.
